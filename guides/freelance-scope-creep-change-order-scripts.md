# Free freelance scope-creep and change-order email scripts

Copy these when a client asks for "just a small add," a new page, another revision round, or work that was never in the proposal. Each script is ready to send. Fill in the bracketed bits, keep the tone calm, and do not start the extra work until you have a written yes.

## How to use these scripts

1. Restate the request in one sentence, using the client's words where you can.
2. Point to a written line in the proposal, statement of work, or last approved email. Do not argue from memory.
3. Offer two or three options. A useful set is: paid change, park it until after the current deadline, or swap something that is not yet built.
4. Name the fee only when you can back it. If the ask is unclear, ask two or three questions and do not invent a number.
5. Say when work starts: after written approval, and after any deposit your terms require.
6. Leave out apologies for having a scope, threats, and essays about "respecting your process."

Subject lines are suggestions. On Slack or WhatsApp, skip the subject and send the body, tightened to a few short paragraphs.

## 1. "Can you also add this? It should be quick"

Use when the client asks for a new page, feature, or deliverable that your proposal excluded, or never named.

Subject: Quick check on the [gift-card page] request

> Hi [Priya],
>
> Happy to look at this. A [Gift Cards page] is new work, so it sits outside the [6 pages] in the approved proposal.
>
> I can still do it before [15 April] if we treat it as a written change.
>
> What I would ship: [a Gift Cards page in the current design, with a button to your existing gift-card link, your copy, and mobile QA].
>
> Fee: [$1,800], with [$900] to start and [$900] when it is on staging. The [15 April] launch date holds if you approve this week and I have [the working link and your copy] within [3 business days].
>
> Two other options if you want to stay on the current fee: we can park this until after launch, or we can drop [the About page, which is not yet built] and build [Gift Cards] instead.
>
> Reply with A (paid change), B (park it), or C (swap [About]), and I will not start the new page until I have that in writing.
>
> [Jordan]

## 2. Extra revision round after the agreed number

Use when the contract includes a fixed number of revision rounds and that number is used up.

Subject: Next revision round for [the homepage]

> Hi [Alex],
>
> I can do another pass on [the homepage]. The agreement includes [two] revision rounds, and we have used both: [the 3 March typography pass and the 10 March layout pass].
>
> This next round is therefore a change. I would need [one more design pass, plus a short round of copy-in and QA], which is [4 to 6 hours].
>
> Fee: [$600], due before I start. I can deliver by [Friday] if you approve today and send [the marked-up PDF or a single list of changes].
>
> If you would rather hold the current fee, we can ship [the homepage as it stands after round two] and leave further polish for a later phase.
>
> Reply "approved" and I will send the invoice, or tell me to hold the current version.
>
> [Jordan]

## 3. New integration or third-party account that was never in scope

Use when the client asks you to connect a tool, payment provider, CRM, or other account that the proposal assumed would already exist, or excluded outright.

Subject: [Toast gift cards] and the current launch scope

> Hi [Priya],
>
> Connecting [Toast gift cards] is new work. The approved proposal covers [a 6-page site] and assumes [you already have a working gift-card link]. It does not include [setup or changes inside Toast].
>
> If the [Toast] link is already live, I can add [a button and a short Gift Cards page] as a paid change: [$1,800], with the same launch date if you approve this week.
>
> If the link is not live yet, I recommend we launch the [6 pages] we already agreed and I quote the [Gift Cards] work as a follow-on. That way the launch is not tied to [Toast] setup I cannot control.
>
> Which of those do you want? I will not start the new page, or any [Toast] configuration, until you reply.
>
> [Jordan]

## 4. New work that would slip the deadline

Use when the ask is real, but doing it now would move a date you have already committed to.

Subject: [Homepage video] and the [15 April] launch date

> Hi [Sam],
>
> A [homepage video edit and captions] is outside the current scope, and I can do it. I cannot also hold [15 April] if we add it this week.
>
> Two clean options:
>
> A. We add the [video] as a paid change of [$1,200], and move launch to [22 April], which gives me time for [edit, captions, and a review round].
> B. We launch on [15 April] with [the stills already approved], and I start the [video] as a follow-on after launch, same fee, with a date we set once the site is live.
>
> I recommend B if [the rest of the site is ready and the video file is still being filmed]. I recommend A if you would rather wait and ship the video on day one.
>
> Reply A or B, and I will update the timeline only after that.
>
> [Jordan]

## 5. "We thought that was included"

Use when the client believes the work is already paid for, and you need to point at the written scope without picking a fight.

Subject: Clarifying what is in the [April] proposal

> Hi [Maya],
>
> Worth a quick clarification, because I want us reading the same document.
>
> The approved proposal covers [a 6-page marketing site, desktop and mobile, with two revision rounds]. It lists these exclusions: [extra pages, a blog, and gift-card merchandising].
>
> [A blog with three launch posts] is therefore new work, not a missed item in the current fee.
>
> If you still want it, I can send a short change order: [what I would write, the fee, and what happens to the date]. If you would rather stay with the current scope, we leave [the blog] for a later phase and I keep going on the [6 pages].
>
> Which would you like me to do?
>
> [Jordan]

## 6. Swap one deliverable for another, same fee

Use when something has not been built yet, and the client would rather have a new item instead of an unused one.

Subject: Swap [About] for [Gift Cards], same fee

> Hi [Priya],
>
> We can keep the current fee if we swap work that I have not started.
>
> [The About page] is not yet built. If you are happy to drop it from this phase, I can build [a Gift Cards page in the current design] in its place, with [a button to your existing link, your copy, and QA]. The [15 April] date still holds if you confirm this week and send [the link and copy] by [Monday].
>
> What would not be included: [setup inside Toast, extra pages, or photography].
>
> If you later want [About] as well, that would be a separate, paid change.
>
> Reply "swap About for Gift Cards" if you want to go ahead, and I will confirm the revised page list in writing before I start.
>
> [Jordan]

## 7. Park it until after launch

Use when the request is valid, but now is the wrong time, or a dependency is still missing.

Subject: Let's park [the gift-card page] until after launch

> Hi [Priya],
>
> I agree [Gift Cards] is worth doing. I do not recommend adding it to this launch.
>
> [The Toast link is not live yet], and the current agreement is [the 6 named pages, launching 15 April]. Adding a page that depends on a setup I cannot finish would put that date at risk.
>
> Proposal: we launch the agreed [6 pages] on [15 April], and I send a separate quote for [the Gift Cards page] the week after launch, once [the link works on a phone].
>
> If the link goes live this week and you still want it in the first release, say so and I will send the paid change-order figures instead.
>
> [Jordan]

## 8. "While you're in there" after launch

Use for a post-launch request that sounds small and is not covered by a support window, or that has used the support window up.

Subject: Post-launch request: [new testimonial row]

> Hi [Chris],
>
> I can add [a third testimonial row on the homepage]. The build phase is closed, and the [14-day] launch-support window covers [bug fixes, not new sections].
>
> This is a small paid change: [2 to 3 hours], fee [$300], including [desktop and mobile QA]. I can stage it within [three business days] of approval and the [new quotes and photos].
>
> If you would rather batch a few post-launch tweaks, send me the full list and I will quote them as one change instead of several small ones.
>
> Work starts after you reply "approved."
>
> [Jordan]

## 9. A new stakeholder adds a new list

Use when someone new joins the thread and treats the project as a blank slate.

Subject: Keeping [the site] to the approved scope

> Hi [both],
>
> Welcome, [Taylor]. To keep us aligned: the approved scope is [a 6-page marketing site, two revision rounds, launch 15 April]. The exclusions are in [section 4 of the proposal], including [extra pages and a members area].
>
> The new items on [Taylor's] list — [a members area and a resource library] — are new work. I can quote them as a follow-on. I cannot add them to the current fee or hold [15 April] if we start them now.
>
> Suggested path: I finish the agreed [6 pages] for [15 April], and I send a separate estimate for [the members area and library] by [next Wednesday], with a start date after launch.
>
> If you want any of those items in the first release, tell me which ones, and I will send a change order and a revised date. I will not start them until that is approved in writing.
>
> [Jordan]

## 10. Retainer hours are used up

Use when you work on a monthly cap and the client sends more work after the hours are gone.

Subject: [March] retainer hours are used, next steps for [the landing page]

> Hi [Devon],
>
> The [March] retainer is [16 hours], and as of [today] those hours are used: [the campaign landing page, the two ad variants, and the email QA], as logged in [the weekly status notes].
>
> [The extra landing-page variant] would be [3 to 4 hours] on top of that.
>
> Options:
>
> A. I do it now as a one-off, fee [$500], and start after you approve.
> B. We put it first in the [April] retainer, which starts [1 April].
> C. We drop [the unused social set still in this month's list] and I use any remaining time, if you want me to re-check the log and confirm what is left. Right now I believe the cap is already reached, so C may not apply.
>
> I recommend B unless the variant has to ship this week. Reply A, B, or C and I will schedule it from there.
>
> [Jordan]

## 11. You already started the unpaid work

Use when you began a "quick favor" and need to stop the leak without blaming the client.

Subject: Pausing [the extra page] until we agree the change

> Hi [Priya],
>
> I started a first pass on [the Gift Cards page] after your note yesterday, then stopped so we do not get ahead of the agreement.
>
> That page is not in the approved [6-page] scope. I have not billed the [two hours] already spent, and I will not keep going until we agree how to handle it.
>
> If you want it in this launch, I can send a change order for the remaining work, fee [$1,500], which assumes I absorb the time already spent. If you would rather park it, I will leave the draft on the side and finish the agreed pages.
>
> Which would you like? I will wait for your written reply.
>
> [Jordan]

## 12. Confirm a verbal yes, and attach the change order

Use after a call where the client said "yes, do it," and you need that in writing before you start.

Subject: Please confirm the change order for [the Gift Cards page]

> Hi [Priya],
>
> Following our call today, here is the change I will start once you confirm.
>
> Request: add [a Gift Cards page] before launch.
> Work included: [one page in the current visual system, desktop and mobile, a button to an existing gift-card URL you provide, copy-in, and QA].
> Still excluded: [setup or changes inside Toast, merchandising, email design, extra pages, photography].
> Fee: [$1,800], with [$900] before work starts and [$900] when the page is on staging.
> Timing: page on staging within [10 business days] of the deposit and a working link. Site launch remains [15 April] if you approve this week.
>
> Reply "approved" to this email and pay the [$900] deposit. I will not start until both are done.
>
> [Jordan]

## A short change-order you can paste under the email

Keep this in the same thread, or as a one-page attachment.

```text
CHANGE ORDER
Project: [Project name]
Date: [Today]
Requested by: [Client name]
Request: [One sentence]
Work included in this change: [List]
Work still excluded: [List]
Time estimate: [Range], capped as a fixed fee or billed hourly, as agreed
Fee: [Amount and currency]
Payment terms: [Deposit and balance, or net terms]
Deadline or milestone: [Date, and what it depends on]
Work starts when: written approval [and the deposit has cleared]
Approval: reply "approved" [and pay the deposit]
```

## What not to send

- A long explanation of how hard your job is.
- A fee you cannot explain. If you need a look at the files first, say that and quote after.
- "Sure, I'll squeeze it in" with no date, no fee, and no approval.
- A threat to stop all work. Pause only the new request, and keep the agreed work moving unless the client has broken a payment term.
- Fake urgency, or a discount you did not decide to offer.
